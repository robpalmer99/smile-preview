One campaign, not three

Three of us produced strong Smile creative inside eighteen hours, on three different premises. Faisa's "Know Your Order" builds every ad out of the customer's own bar order. James's five images headline "Finally, a real cocktail." Our own seven statics led with the moment. Faisa's deck opens by arguing that James's headline doesn't work, and she has ~770 reviews behind her. All three sets are good. What follows makes them one campaign before any more of it gets filmed or printed.

The rule: claim layering

"Real vodka, not malt" is the only hard, checkable thing Smile owns, so it stays in every ad. It just stops being the headline. Every unit now has three layers, and the claim lives in the second one.

1 · The orderThe ticket and a one-read joke, in words a customer would actually use. Its only job is to stop the thumb. No brand adjectives, ever.
2 · The check"Real vodka, not malt. Flip the can: ours says Made with Vodka." First line of the caption, or the 3-to-5-second beat of a film.
3 · The mandatoriesReal vodka · 7% ABV · Please drink responsibly · 21+ · Find it near you.

In one line: never let the brand say "real" before the customer has said her order. And wherever the proof can be an instruction rather than a claim, make it one. Hopkins didn't claim Pepsodent removed the film, he told readers to run their tongue over their teeth and check. Smile sells in about 370 stores and nowhere online, so every shopper who reads the ad is a shopper who will shortly be within arm's reach of the evidence.

Ten statics, one ticket

The seven existing photographs rebuilt, plus three new units that close the shelf gap. The headline is now a joke about one specific can, the order ticket is the signature on every unit, and "Know your order." closes all ten. Seven cost nothing to rebuild; the two new scenes came in at well under a dollar of the approved AI budget, and the proof unit is Smile's own photography.

James's five, re-layered — his own body lines promoted

Ours is real vodka, cranberry, lime and soda. 3 · The Espresso Martini — "Someone already made it." James wrote this as his opening body line. It's the strongest sentence in his set and it was sitting under the weakest. 4 · The Text Thread — "He can't make one. He can open one." His line with the first mention cut, because the picture says which drink. 5 · The Shaker — "Everything a bartender does. Done." Again his own body line promoted. The full stop in the middle does the work. One gap to fix: three of the five are espresso martini. The device needs spreading across Spicy Mule, Pineapple Orange and Lemonade Tea so the set covers the shelf.

The proof unit was specced wrong, and the correction made it better

It was going to be "Don't take our word for it. Turn it around." — a hand rotating a can to show MADE WITH VODKA on the back. Going back to Smile's own photography shows that is the wrong way round. MADE WITH VODKA is printed on the front, directly above the flavour name. It is the competitors who make you turn the can over, because that is where "flavored malt beverage" is hiding. So the unit is now "We put it on the front.", over Smile's own macro, with a bracket under the printed line. It is a better ad, it needed no generation at all, and it carries no risk of a garbled label — the one thing this particular unit could not survive. It is also, it turns out, exactly the argument the Read the Can film has been making since v1, without anyone having noticed it was the same point.

Two things that need Ben's call

The Platinum medal has come off the universal badge strip. One medal was awarded, and carrying it on all seven of the old units implied each flavour had won one. It now appears on the Pineapple Orange unit only. Worth confirming the award's flavour with Smile, though it no longer blocks anything: the Read the Can film already holds on the Pineapple Orange can through its Platinum beat, so the film and the static agree. "Wake me up and turn me up" is retired. It reads as an intoxication cue, which DISCUS guidance rules out. The espresso unit now runs "We did the hard part. You find a glass." That was a compliance fix as much as a register one.

What this does to the films

Faisa's six Reels run 6 seconds on four beats. Ours run 20 seconds with a story in them, because that's what Ben asked for on 28 September and because a story needs the air. Both are right, but if they share nothing, Smile sees two campaigns. So ours keep the story and borrow her top and tail. Every film now opens on the order printing out of a receipt printer (0 to 2s) and closes on the same lockup: "Know your order. Find Smile near you." What happens in between is either six seconds of joke or twenty seconds of story. "Read the Can" is promoted rather than cut — under the rule above it's the one film allowed to lead with the claim, because "flip the can" is something the viewer can go and do. It's the designated proof film. And Ben's own bartender script lands on "Somebody already did the hard part" at 11 seconds. The V7 static above reaches "We did the hard part. You find a glass." Those were written five days and two people apart and arrived in the same place, which is the best evidence we have that the register is right.

07The quiz funnel — "Find your cocktail"

The centrepiece. An ad lands here, four taps later the visitor has chosen their drink rather than been sold one, and the payoff is a cocktail card — not a can. Why it's theirs, what's actually in it, and how to serve it properly: the right glass, the ice, the garnish, crack and pour. Then, and only then, the nearest store.

The last question is the one that does the work. Smile's five cans map one-to-one onto five classic bar orders, so we ask what you'd order — and the real-cocktail argument gets made in the visitor's own words, with no ingredient lecture anywhere on the page. A sixth answer, "honestly, all of them", lands on the variety pack.

Take the quiz

Built mobile-first — every question sits above the fold on a phone. Running on the same live retail data as the finder, with get-directions as the tracked conversion, a QR code so desktop visitors can carry the store to their phone, and an email capture that only ever appears after the result.

12How we'll know the ads are working

Smile sells through retail shelves, not a checkout page, so no one can honestly trace a click to a till receipt. Instead, every step a customer takes on the way to the store leaves a mark we can count — and every number ties back to the ad and region that produced it.

The page is the instrumentEvery ad carries its own tag. Per ad, per region: arrivals, finder searches, and "Directions" taps — someone one decision from the shelf. That's the primary signal, weekly.
The $2 couponEmail for $2 off a first 4-pack, tied to the chosen store with an expiry. Redemptions are countable purchases — and the list is Smile's to keep.
QR codesEvery video end card, shelf talker and festival banner gets its own code, so a scan says which creative moved them — into the same weekly numbers.
One question at signup"Where did you first hear about Smile?" The cheapest tracking tool in advertising, and it catches what pixels miss.
Brand search by regionAds run in San Diego, searches rise in San Diego but not Sacramento — the ads are landing, clicks or not.
The monthly needleSmile's sell-through by region laid over the spend map: are advertised regions outgrowing the rest? Directional, presented that way, and over a quarter the cleanest read there is.

The findingEvery major RTD brand runs zero Meta video. The winners are small independents on year-old creative. The feed is there to be owned.
